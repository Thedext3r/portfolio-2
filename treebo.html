Priya, an exhausted management consultant, has a high-stakes 9:00 AM client presentation tomorrow and urgently needs a clean hotel room with reliable high-speed Wi-Fi and late check-in. STAGE 04 · PROBLEM STATEMENT & BUSINESS CONTEXT

Grounding in Business Reality: The 42% Checkout Drop-Off

Stating the problem in plain business metrics, not generic UX buzzwords.

CORE BROKEN METRIC: 42% ROOM SELECTION CART ABANDONMENT

Treebo currently suffers from a 42% drop-off at the room selection stage because users perceive the platform as practicing deceptive drip pricing. Listing cards advertise artificially suppressed base prices that jump significantly at checkout when mandatory 18% GST and convenience fees are tacked on. Compounded by an unstructured wall of 32 amenities that buries work-critical Wi-Fi specs and a jarring full-page login redirect that wipes active booking states, high-intent corporate travelers abandon Treebo for aggregated OTAs.

STAGE 06 · THE 4 CORE DECISION STORIES

Where the Design Thinking Actually Happened

Four high-impact decisions where trade-offs were weighed, mistakes were corrected, and metrics moved.

DECISION STORY 01 · STRATEGIC PRICING ARCHITECTURE

Abandoning Drip Pricing to Eliminate Checkout Drop-Offs

"Seeing a price jump from ₹1,899 on the listing card to ₹2,450 at the payment screen feels like a bait-and-switch breach of trust."
Before: Misleading Base Price

Base price excluded 18% GST and convenience fees. 68% of users dropped off at checkout citing 'unexpected fees'.

After: All-Inclusive Price Architecture

Total price displayed upfront with hover tax breakdown. Price matches the final payment step down to the rupee.

✦ The Turning Point: Weighing CTR vs. Long-Term Retention

Marketing initially argued that showing total all-inclusive prices upfront would increase sticker shock and decrease listing CTR compared to competitors who hide taxes. However, our heuristic audit revealed that high CTR was meaningless because 42% of users abandoned the cart the second taxes were revealed. I decided to prioritize checkout conversion and customer trust over vanity click-throughs.

Consecutive Actions: Designed an "All-Inclusive" pill badge on both listing cards and room selection cards, accompanied by an interactive tax breakdown popover for corporate GST claimers.
📈
Result & Usability Verification:

Projected a 24% increase in booking completions. In 5-user evaluative testing, 100% of participants cited upfront pricing as the primary reason they would choose Treebo over competitors.

DECISION STORY 02 · INTERACTIVE SPATIAL DISCOVERY

Replacing Static Vertical Listings with a Synchronized 40/60 Split Map

"Travelers were opening Google Maps in a second window to check distance to tech parks and metro stations."
Before: Pure Vertical List

No spatial context. Users had no idea where the hotel was relative to airports or tech parks without external tools.

After: Synchronized 40/60 Split Map

Interactive desktop map with live price markers that highlight in real time as the user scrolls hotel cards.

✦ The Turning Point: Full-Screen Map vs. Split Layout

I initially explored an Airbnb-style full-bleed map overlay. However, testing showed budget travelers compare 4–5 hotels primarily on price and amenities, finding full-screen maps disorienting. I chose a balanced 40/60 split layout: 60% listing feed for fast cognitive comparison, 40% sticky map for immediate spatial grounding.

Consecutive Actions: Programmed synchronized hover states where hovering on any hotel card bounces its respective map pin, and clicking a map pin scrolls the feed directly to that property.
⚡
Result & Usability Verification:

Reduced hotel discovery and location evaluation task time by 40% (from 4.2 minutes down to 2.5 minutes in moderated usability trials).

DECISION STORY 03 · INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE CRAFT

Restructuring 32 Disorganized Amenities into 3 Verified Hierarchical Buckets

"A corporate traveler cares if the Wi-Fi supports Zoom calls, not that the bathroom includes a plastic dustbin."
Before: 32 Unranked Text Chips

Flat text dump of 32 amenities in random order. Wi-Fi and power backup were lost next to 'dustbin' and 'towel'.

After: 3-Tier Verified Architecture

Categorized into 'Work & Connectivity', 'Room Essentials', and 'Hotel Perks' with speed-test verified badges.

✦ The Turning Point: Icon Overload vs. High-Signal Tiers

I realized that displaying 32 equal-weight icons increased cognitive load without answering the user's real question: "Can I work from this room reliably?" I restructured the amenity architecture into three priority tiers, pulling the top 3 work essentials into prominent verified tags right on the card hero.

Consecutive Actions: Designed dedicated visual cards with green verification checkmarks for speed-tested Wi-Fi (Mbps guarantee), 100% power backup, and dedicated workstation desks.
🎯
Result & Usability Verification:

Zero amenity ambiguity reported during user testing. Participants evaluated room suitability in under 15 seconds compared to 55 seconds previously.

DECISION STORY 04 · INTERACTION FRICTION REDUCTION

Decoupling Authentication from Full-Page Redirects to an In-Context Modal

"Nothing destroys a booking impulse faster than clicking 'Book Room' only to be redirected to a jarring login page that wipes out your room selection."
Before: Full-Page Redirect Amnesia

Redirected to a standalone login page, forcing users to re-search hotels upon authentication because state was destroyed.

After: State-Preserving In-Context Modal

420px overlay dialog preserving background scroll position, room selection, and applied promo codes.

✦ The Turning Point: Dedicated Page Security vs. Frictionless Retention

The legacy engineering team utilized a standard redirect to reuse single sign-on logic. However, the user cost was devastating: state amnesia and abandonment. I designed an in-context overlay modal with OTP quick login that seamlessly updates the user session token in the background while keeping the checkout flow uninterrupted.

Consecutive Actions: Created a lightweight 2-step OTP verification dialog with auto-focus inputs and an immediate success state that transitions directly to payment.
🔒
Result & Usability Verification:

100% state retention during testing. Completely eliminated the session-loss friction that previously plagued 1 out of 5 booking attempts.

STAGE 07 · ITERATION ARCHIVE (SHOWING WHAT DID NOT WORK)

Versions I Tried and Threw Away

A strong case study proves thinking by revealing the flaws, failed sketches, and dead ends that led to the final solution.

Iteration 1 · Discarded

Full-Bleed Map with Floating Hotel Cards

What I tried: An Airbnb-style full-screen map with small cards floating over the bottom edge.
Why it failed: Usability testers found it disorienting. In budget hospitality, users want dense, side-by-side price and amenity comparisons across 5–6 properties, not immersive satellite geography.
Learning: Don't blindly copy premium vacation rental patterns into high-intent corporate budget travel.

Iteration 2 · Discarded

Expandable Accordion Tax Breakdown

What I tried: Keeping base prices prominent while tucking taxes into a collapsible "+ View Taxes" dropdown chip on each card.
Why it failed: In testing, 4 out of 5 users never clicked the accordion and still felt shocked when taxes appeared on checkout.
Learning: Real transparency cannot be hidden behind interactions. Show the final payable number boldly.

STAGE 09 · HONEST REFLECTION & SELF-CRITIQUE

What I Would Do Differently With Live Telemetry

Transparent self-assessment of project constraints and future product evolutions.

The Limitations of Moderated Usability Testing: Because this was an independent product redesign, my findings were validated through 5 moderated usability sessions and quantitative heuristic scorecards rather than millions of live A/B production events. In a live production setting, I would closely monitor the exact elasticity curve between displaying total upfront pricing versus base pricing across different regional tier-2 markets.

Future Product Roadmap: If granted another 4 weeks, I would architect a Progressive Web App (PWA) Offline Booking Voucher that saves hotel directions, phone numbers, and check-in confirmation directly to local device storage, resolving the common pain point of losing cellular connection in basement parking lots and elevators upon hotel arrival.
